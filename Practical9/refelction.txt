Overall, this practical was moderately challenging but highly rewarding. Implementing 
the basic stochastic SIR model and the vaccination model was relatively straightforward 
once I understood how to use 'np.random.choice' to assign states based on probabilities. 
I particularly enjoyed the vaccination section because visualizing the different infection 
curves provided a very clear, intuitive understanding of "herd immunity".

The most difficult part of the practical was building the 2D spatial SIR model. Managing 
the grid boundaries, ensuring neighbors were correctly identified without causing 
index-out-of-bounds errors, and logically tracking the states with np.argwhere required careful thought.

The most important thing I learned today is how to encode real-world, dynamic probabilistic 
behaviors into array-based mathematical models using Python. It was interesting to simulate 
a spatial outbreak and watch the infection spread and eventually halt as the population gained resistance.
